A plain move takes the direct path, like Stronghold. Shift and Alt are the two options on top: a hand-drawn route of waypoints, and a march that gives enemies a wide berth. A cautious unit keeps re-planning as it goes — send it off on a clear road and it will still bend away from an ambush that only reveals itself later. It steers around foes you can see, so it reacts the moment one comes into view.

IV Train

Every soldier is an armed peasant — keep some idle.

Click barracksOpen the training panel
Unit buttonRecruit one — costs wood & a spare idle peasant
RosterSoldier all-rounder · Runner fast striker · Brute tanky · Archer ranged · Scout the recon unit

The Scout is your eyes: the fastest legs on the field, a far-seeing eye (it lights nearly twice a soldier's fog radius), and stealth — the enemy can't make it out until they're nearly on top of it, so it slips through the fog unseen. But it's frail and feeble in a fight — send it to find the enemy, not to trade blows. Its wide sight also feeds the cautious march: what a scout reveals, your armies will route around.

Build a woodcutter, quarry and iron mine by the trees, rock & rusty ore — each hires its own worker. For food, see the Food Cycle; a house raises how many people you can hold. Any building that wants a worker and can't find a spare peasant shows a ⚠ until one is free.

V Food Cycle

Food is what grows your town — and a farm makes it straight from the land, no chain to staff.

FarmSows a field and reaps Food directly — one farmer, one field, no milling or baking. Any open ground grows a field (yield 1), so you're never short of space — but the richer soil scattered across your land, and in patches out on your flanks, reaps 2 or 3. Pick the Farm (or press V) to see it; aim your fields at the 3s. A field can't sit on wood, stone or iron.
GranaryA food drop-off by the fields — like the storehouse is for timber. Cuts the haul home, so food banks faster.
KeepRations serve that food each turn → Approval → newcomers arrive (see Realm).

Field → Food → mouths fed. A farm needs a spare peasant to work it — if none is free, a ⚠ flags the building until one is. A few farms on good soil feed a sizeable town, so plant early and let a food buffer build before you raise rations.

VI Realm

Two dials share one number — Approval. Keep it above 50% and newcomers arrive; let it sink and idlers drift off (workers always stay).

Click keepOpen the realm panel — the two dials with ◀ ▶ buttons and every effect spelled out (gold per head, approval per turn)
-·=Tax down · up — higher fills the treasury but costs approval; the lowest steps pay a bribe that buys it back
[·]Rations down · up — a fuller table wins approval but eats more food each turn; an empty larder always starves the realm, whatever the order

Both dials move approval on the same ±9 scale, so they cancel step for step: crank tax to fill the treasury, then set a richer table to buy the approval back — you trade gold against food.

You always buy high and sell low — that ±25% gap is the merchant's cut, so churning a good back and forth just bleeds gold. A standing order is where the market earns its keep: set auto-buy weapons and your tax gold quietly becomes arms while your barracks turns them into soldiers — a war economy that runs itself. And when you need an army now, hire mercenaries — the population gate that limits a trained army does not apply to gold.

Fair play — why gold can't buy the game

  • Wages, not just a price. Every mercenary draws pay each turn (≈ hire price ÷ 50 — Soldier 2g, Archer 3g, Brute 4g) and deserts if you can't pay, so your army is capped by sustainable income, not your hoard. The market panel shows your running wage bill.
  • It fights your own crown. Those wages drain the very treasury the Economic path races to 70,000 — a standing army or the gold win, not both.
  • Anyone can hire. The market is open to every path, and tax scales with population (which the 200-pop win-floor guarantees everyone), so a big-population realm can out-earn a merchant.
  • Rivals can bleed it. Every merc cut down is gold you must re-spend — an expensive company is a target, not a wall.
  • An army is a tool, not a path. Troops defend, raid and annex, but you still win by a path (gold, faith, wonders, land) — never by force alone.

VIII Paths to Victory

More than one way to win — a scored race, not just the last keep standing. Take the HIGH goal of one path and the MEDIUM of a different path, each held for a spell. Cross 80% of any HIGH goal and the whole realm is told — the window for a rival to strike back.

First, be a kingdom. No crown counts until your realm holds at least 200 people — a floor under every path, so you can't snap a quick win from a hamlet. Meet a goal while too small and the tracker shows ⚠ needs 200 pop; the hold only begins once you've grown (build houses and keep approval high to settle them).

IX The Tech Tree

The tree is how you win. A realm banks research every turn (top bar); press C and spend it, node by node, to climb a branch. Each branch's ★ capstone unlocks its crown — and you can capstone only one branch, while a second branch costs ever more. So one branch to its capstone + a shallow dip into another is the dual-goal.

TrunkRoads (faster research) opens the five gates — Chapel (faith), Market (gold), Scholar's Hut (science), Farmstead (census), Muster (spies & war).
ForkEach branch has a pick-one sidegrade — taking one closes the other.
WonderThe Science crown you build: unlocked by the Academy, a grand monument that rises over ~30s — visible & sabotageable the whole time, counting only once finished. Each costs more than the last.
⚔ War-toolDeep in each branch, a node that makes war feed that crown — loot, materials, faith, or conquest (see Spies & War).

Religious: a church converts your people (faith opens at 25%, a small dense town converts fastest). Economic: the branch pays a trade income on top of tax. Science: research speeds itself, then the Academy raises wonders. Domain: Homesteads swell your housing, and Provincial Keeps let you build a new keep — each a fresh territory (found five for the crown).

X Spies & War

Every crown is announced at 80% — and a spy is the one thing that pushes a rival's score back. Research a dagger in the Spy Guild (the shared war branch — any path can reach it), then press X and loose it at your rival. Each costs gold and rests on a cooldown.

Notice the counters: a branch's own Tier-III node hardens it against the dagger meant for it — so climbing your crown makes you tougher to knock off it, and the Bodyguard stops the Assassin cold.

War feeds the attacker. Research your branch's ⚔ war-tool and every fight fuels your crown: Privateers pillage gold, War Loot strips wood & stone for wonders, a Crusade emboldens the faith — and Conquest annexes an enemy keep (its land and its people) instead of razing it.

Know your enemy. Computer opponents chase crowns too — several bots spread across the paths, each climbing its branch and wielding its war-tool. A Hard bot also runs the spy ring: it locks a dagger onto you the moment you're announced at 80% and keeps that score suppressed, and it rushes a Bodyguard if it sees you train an Assassin.

You cannot kill the king. Raze a realm's last keep and its ruler is not eliminated — the king flees into exile, the fallen realm is razed and reset, and after a short regroup a new keep rises at the map's safest, most distant corner. A brutal setback, never a death: the exile loses their buildings, treasury and victory holds (you keep the loot), but keeps their researched knowledge and any banked medium — so a beaten rival can claw back, and no early rush kingmakes anyone out of the game. And landing that blow is dangerous: the instant the keep falls, the exiled king's champion — the Avenger (600 hp, a battering blow, fast) — rises from the ruins in the attacker's midst. Exile-only, never trained or hired: often the wiser play is to leave a rival at one keep than hand them a monster.
